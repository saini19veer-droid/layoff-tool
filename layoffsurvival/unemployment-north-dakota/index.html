How North Dakota Unemployment Benefits Are Calculated

Base period: first 4 of last 5 completed quarters. Benefit = 60% of average weekly wage up to maximum.

North Dakota pays 60% of average weekly wages and has a $633 maximum — high for a rural state.

Eligibility Requirements in North Dakota

To qualify for unemployment benefits in North Dakota, you must meet all of these conditions:

  • Unemployed through no fault of your own — laid off, position eliminated, or company closed. Voluntary resignation or termination for cause generally disqualifies you.
  • Earned sufficient wages during the base period — North Dakota looks at your earnings in the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters before you filed.
  • Able and available to work — you must be actively seeking employment and available to accept suitable work if offered.
  • Registered with North Dakota workforce services — most states require you to register with their job search system as a condition of receiving benefits.
File immediately — every day costs you money North Dakota has a 1-week waiting period before your first payment — but that clock starts on the day you file. File at File at jobsnd.com the same day you are laid off, or the next morning at the latest. Every day you delay is potentially lost benefits you cannot recover.

How to File for Unemployment in North Dakota

  • File online at File at jobsnd.com — this is the fastest method and available 24/7.
  • Have ready: your Social Security number, employer name and address, start and end dates of employment, and reason for separation.
  • Report your severance — if you received severance pay, you must report it. North Dakota may treat it as wages which could delay the start of your benefits.
  • Certify weekly — after filing, you must certify your job search activity every week to continue receiving payments. Missing a certification week can pause your benefits.
  • Report any income — if you earn money through freelancing, part-time work, or gig work while collecting benefits, you must report it. Most states reduce your benefit by the amount earned over a small threshold.
Taxes on unemployment benefits Unemployment benefits are taxable as ordinary income at the federal level. You can request 10% federal withholding when you file — highly recommended. If you don't withhold, you will owe the taxes when you file your return, which can be a painful surprise when you are already financially stretched.

How North Dakota Compares to Other States

North Dakota sits in the middle tier of US unemployment programs. At $633 maximum weekly benefit and 60% wage replacement, the program is around the national median. States like Massachusetts ($1,033), Washington ($999), and Connecticut ($803) pay significantly more, while Florida ($275) and Mississippi ($235) pay much less.
