How Wyoming Unemployment Benefits Are Calculated

Base period: the first 4 of the last 5 completed calendar quarters before you file. Your weekly benefit is 4% of your earnings in your highest-paid base-period quarter — roughly 52% wage replacement for a typical earner — capped at $671, with a $48 floor. Your total benefit for the year is capped at the lesser of 26 times your weekly benefit or 30% of your base-period wages, so your actual number of weeks depends on your earnings history, not a flat number for everyone. Wyoming has a 1-week waiting period.

Eligibility Requirements in Wyoming

To qualify for unemployment benefits in Wyoming, you must meet all of these conditions:

  • Unemployed through no fault of your own — laid off, position eliminated, or company closed. Voluntary resignation or termination for cause generally disqualifies you.
  • Earned sufficient wages during the base period — Wyoming looks at your earnings in the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters before you filed.
  • Able and available to work — you must be actively seeking employment and available to accept suitable work if offered.
  • Registered for work with Wyoming DWS — through the state job board (now HireWYO) — and filing weekly continued claims through the WYUI portal.
  • Certify weekly — claim weeks end on a Saturday, and you must file within 14 days of that end date to stay on track. State rules also require contacting at least two employers each week and keeping a written record.
File immediately — every day costs you money Wyoming has a 1-week unpaid waiting week, and you cannot serve it until you have filed and started claiming weeks. File at wyui.wyo.gov the same day you are laid off, or the next morning at the latest — every week you delay pushes your first payment back by a week you cannot recover.

How to File for Unemployment in Wyoming

  • File online at wyui.wyo.gov, the Wyoming Unemployment Insurance user portal — this is the fastest method and available 24/7.
  • Have ready: your Social Security number, employer name and address, start and end dates of employment, and reason for separation. If you worked outside Wyoming in the last 18 months, have those employers' names and dates ready too.
  • Report your severance — if you received severance pay, you must report it. Wyoming may treat it as wages which could delay the start of your benefits.
  • Certify weekly — claim weeks end on a Saturday, and you must file within 14 days of that end date to be considered timely.
  • Report any income — report gross earnings for the week you worked, not the week you were paid. Earning more than half your weekly benefit amount reduces your payment; earning at or above your full weekly benefit amount, or working 35 or more hours, means no benefit for that week.
Taxes on unemployment benefits Unemployment benefits are taxable as ordinary income at the federal level. You can request 10% federal withholding when you file — highly recommended. If you don't withhold, you will owe the taxes when you file your return, which can be a painful surprise when you are already financially stretched.

What's Different About Filing in Wyoming

A few things are worth knowing about Wyoming specifically:

  • Your total benefit isn't a flat 26 weeks for everyone. Wyoming caps your year's total at the lesser of 26 times your weekly benefit or 30% of your base-period wages. The 30% limit is what bites, and it bites when your earnings were bunched into one or two quarters rather than spread across the year — seasonal and part-year work, not low pay as such. Spread your wages evenly and you reach 26 weeks regardless of how much you earned.
  • Lawmakers have repeatedly tried to shorten this. Three bills since 2023 would have cut the maximum from 26 to 20 times your weekly benefit — SF0138 (2023), SF0175 (2025) and HB0006 (2026) — and all have failed. The most recent failed introduction in the Wyoming House 29-33 on February 9, 2026. Worth checking dws.wyo.gov if you're filing well after this was written, in case a future attempt succeeds.
  • The dollar figures reset every July. The $671 maximum and $48 minimum took effect July 5, 2026, and are published in DWS's own claimant FAQ. Both are recalculated each year from Wyoming's average weekly wage — the maximum is set at 55% of the statewide weekly wage — so if you are reading this after the next July reset, check dws.wyo.gov for the updated figure.
  • Benefits arrive on a prepaid card. Wyoming pays claimants through a U.S. Bank ReliaCard rather than depositing to your own account by default, so factor a few extra days into your first-payment planning. And report earnings carefully — overpayments have to be repaid, and benefits obtained through false information are a disqualifying offence.

How Wyoming Compares to Other States

Wyoming's $671 maximum sits below both of its larger neighbours — Colorado's benefits estimator currently caps the weekly amount at $884, and Utah's maximum is higher than Wyoming's too. Its up-to-26-week duration matches the national standard most states use, a duration several Wyoming lawmakers have tried and failed to shorten in recent years. Because every state resets its cap on its own schedule, check the other state's agency directly before comparing.
