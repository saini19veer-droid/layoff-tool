How Colorado Unemployment Benefits Are Calculated

Base period: total base-period wages of at least 40× your weekly benefit amount, or $2,500, whichever is greater. Colorado calculates your benefit two different ways and pays whichever is higher — 60% of your average weekly wage across your two highest quarters, or 50% of your total base-period earnings divided across the year — both subject to a cap tied to the state's average wage. The result is a maximum of $844. Colorado has no waiting period.

Eligibility Requirements in Colorado

To qualify for unemployment benefits in Colorado, you must meet all of these conditions:

  • Unemployed through no fault of your own — laid off, position eliminated, or company closed. Voluntary resignation or termination for cause generally disqualifies you.
  • Earned sufficient wages during the base period — Colorado looks at your earnings in the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters before you filed.
  • Able and available to work — you must be actively seeking employment and available to accept suitable work if offered.
  • Registered with Colorado workforce services — most states require you to register with their job search system as a condition of receiving benefits.
File immediately — every day costs you money Colorado eliminated its waiting week in 2025, so your paid eligible weeks start counting the day you file — not the day you were laid off. File at cdle.colorado.gov the same day you are laid off, or the next morning at the latest. Every day you delay is a day of benefits you cannot recover.

How Much You Can Earn While Still Collecting in Colorado

You can earn up to 50% of your weekly benefit amount without any reduction — confirmed directly against CDLE's own guidance. Earnings above that reduce your benefit dollar-for-dollar. You also have to work fewer than 32 hours to qualify for a partial payment at all. CDLE's own example, on a $100 WBA: earn $20 in a week and you still get the full $100; earn $60 and you get $90 ($10 over the $50 threshold, deducted); earn $110 or more and you get $0 for that week.

How to File for Unemployment in Colorado

  • File online at cdle.colorado.gov through MyUI+ — this is the fastest method and available 24/7.
  • Verify your identity with ID.me — required for all claimants, so have a photo ID and your phone ready.
  • Have ready: your Social Security number, employer name and address, start and end dates of employment, and reason for separation.
  • Report your severance — if you received severance pay, you must report it. Colorado may treat it as wages which could delay the start of your benefits.
  • Certify weekly — after filing, you must certify your job search activity every week to continue receiving payments. Missing a certification week can pause your benefits.
  • Report any income — if you earn money through freelancing, part-time work, or gig work while collecting benefits, you must report it. Most states reduce your benefit by the amount earned over a small threshold.
Taxes on unemployment benefits Unemployment benefits are taxable as ordinary income at the federal level. You can request 10% federal withholding when you file — highly recommended. If you don't withhold, you will owe the taxes when you file your return, which can be a painful surprise when you are already financially stretched.

What's Different About Filing in Colorado

A few things are distinctly Colorado:

  • You automatically get whichever of two formulas pays more. Formula 1 takes your two best consecutive quarters, divides by 26, and multiplies by 0.60. Formula 2 takes your total base-period wages, divides by 52, then by 2. CDLE runs both and pays the higher result — you don't have to request this.
  • Both the qualifying minimum and every cap reset every July 1, tied to Colorado's average wage growth — so the numbers on this page are current only until next summer.
  • The 50% earnings disregard is unusually generous — many states cap it at 20-25%.
  • No dependent allowance — your benefit is based solely on wage history, unlike Ohio or New Jersey.
  • No waiting week — Colorado permanently eliminated it under SB22-234 once the UI trust fund hit $1 billion in Q2 2025. Your first eligible week is paid, not withheld.
  • ID.me identity verification is mandatory for every claimant filing through MyUI+.

How Colorado Compares to Other States

Colorado's $844 maximum ranks among the more generous programs in the country, well ahead of neighboring Arizona ($320) and just above Utah's $806, though it trails Minnesota's $857 and the top-tier states like Washington and Massachusetts.
