How Florida Unemployment Benefits Are Calculated

Base period: at least $3,400 in wages, spread across at least two calendar quarters, with total wages of at least 1.5 times your highest quarter. Your weekly benefit is your highest-quarter wages divided by 26, capped at $275. Duration is currently a flat 12 weeks — the statutory floor, locked in while Florida's unemployment rate stays under 5%. It only moves up from there (an extra week per 0.5-point rise in the rate), never below 12.

Eligibility Requirements in Florida

To qualify for unemployment benefits in Florida, you must meet all of these conditions:

  • Unemployed through no fault of your own — laid off, position eliminated, or company closed. Voluntary resignation or termination for cause generally disqualifies you.
  • Earned sufficient wages during the base period — Florida looks at your earnings in the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters before you filed.
  • Able and available to work — you must be actively seeking employment and available to accept suitable work if offered.
  • Registered with Florida workforce services — most states require you to register with their job search system as a condition of receiving benefits.
File immediately — every day costs you money Florida has a 1-week waiting period before your first payment — but that clock starts on the day you file. File at connect.myflorida.com the same day you are laid off, or the next morning at the latest. Every day you delay is potentially lost benefits you cannot recover.

How Much You Can Earn While Still Collecting in Florida

Florida's disregard is a fixed dollar figure, not a percentage: you can earn up to $58/week (8× the federal minimum wage) with no reduction. Earn more than that, and it's subtracted from your benefit dollar-for-dollar. Work 32+ hours in a week, or earn at or above your full weekly benefit amount, and you get $0 for that week. Example: a $200 WBA claimant who earns $120 in a week receives $200 − ($120 − $58) = $138.

How to File for Unemployment in Florida

  • File online at connect.myflorida.com through Reconnect — this is the standard, fastest method.
  • Register with Employ Florida as part of your application — this is required, not optional.
  • Have ready: your Social Security number, employer name and address, start and end dates of employment, and reason for separation.
  • Report your severance — if you received severance pay, you must report it. Florida may treat it as wages which could delay the start of your benefits.
  • Certify every two weeks — not weekly. You'll log into Reconnect and confirm you're still unemployed and actively searching.
  • Contact 5 employers per week (3 in small counties) and log each contact in Reconnect — name, location, and date. FloridaCommerce audits these logs, and fabricated contacts are treated as fraud.
  • Report any income — if you earn money through freelancing, part-time work, or gig work while collecting benefits, you must report it. Florida reduces your benefit by earnings over $58/week.
Taxes on unemployment benefits Unemployment benefits are taxable as ordinary income at the federal level. You can request 10% federal withholding when you file — highly recommended. If you don't withhold, you will owe the taxes when you file your return, which can be a painful surprise when you are already financially stretched.

What's Different About Filing in Florida

A few things make Florida's system stand out from most other states — not in a good way:

  • The $275 cap hasn't moved since 2011. A 2022 bill (SB 1568/HB 1489) would have raised it to $375 and extended the 12-week floor to 14. It never made it out of committee.
  • The work-search bar is unusually high. You must contact 5 different prospective employers every week (3 in smaller counties) and log each one in Reconnect. Repeating the same employer three weeks running doesn't count unless they're actively hiring.
  • "Suitable work" gets broader the longer you're on benefits. After 25 weeks in a benefit year, any minimum-wage job paying at least 120% of your weekly benefit amount counts as suitable — turning down that kind of offer can end your claim.
  • You only serve the waiting week once per benefit year, even if your claim lapses and you have to reopen it later in the same year.
  • Real replacement is often far below the "~50%" headline rate. That figure only holds near the bottom of the wage scale — someone earning $50,000/year gets $275/week against a $962/week wage, roughly 29% replacement, because the benefit is capped well before wages are.

How Florida Compares to Other States

Florida pays the lowest maximum weekly benefit of any large state at $275 — a cap unchanged since 2011 — and currently caps duration at a flat 12 weeks, far short of the 26-week standard most states offer. A 2022 bill to raise the cap to $375 and extend the floor to 14 weeks didn't pass; $275 and 12 weeks remain the law.
